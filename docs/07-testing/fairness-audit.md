# Fairness audit

Generated by `python -m src.evaluation.fairness` (raw output in
`ml/artifacts/fairness.json`, not committed). It scores the **held-out test
split** (12,599 patients, 1,179 readmissions) with the promoted model, XGBoost
v2026.09.30.0717 at threshold 0.1012, and compares recall (the share of true
readmissions the model flags) across groups.

Race and gender are **not inputs** to the model. The audit still slices by them,
because leaving them out does not prevent unequal outcomes: other features can
stand in for them.

Overall: recall 0.501, observed readmission rate 9.36%, mean predicted 9.21%.

## Gender

| Group | n | Recall (95% CI) | Flagged | Observed / expected |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Female | 6,667 | 0.551 (0.51-0.59) | 34.6% | 0.99 |
| Male | 5,932 | 0.446 (0.41-0.49) | 31.2% | 1.04 |

**A real gap of 10.5 points.** The confidence intervals do not overlap. Men are
flagged less often *and* readmit slightly more than predicted (1.04). Matching
overall recall for men would need a cutoff of 0.0905, which flags 37.7% of men
instead of 31.2% and lowers precision from 0.134 to 0.125.

Gap between the two groups large enough to measure: 2.2 points, within noise.
The four small groups have too few readmissions (2 to 25) to say anything; they
are shown, not interpreted.

## Age

Recall rises with age: 0.340 (40-50), 0.428 (50-60), 0.475 (60-70), 0.569
(70-80), 0.571 (80-90). The model is least sensitive for the 40-50 group (gap
of 16 points from the overall figure); matching overall recall there would need
a cutoff of 0.0795 and would flag 34.2% instead of 19.0% of that group.

Part of this is base rates: older patients readmit more, so a single cutoff flags
more of them. Part is the model. The audit cannot separate the two.

## What this does and does not mean

- The model is **well calibrated in every group large enough to test** (observed
  over expected 0.91-1.07). It is not systematically over- or under-predicting for
  women, men, or the two large race groups.
- Equal calibration and equal recall cannot both hold when base rates differ; this
  model chose calibration, and the gender and age recall gaps are the result.
- The alternative, group-specific cutoffs, would equalise recall at the cost of
  flagging more low-risk patients in some groups. That is a clinical and ethical
  trade-off between missed readmissions and wasted follow-up capacity, and
  it is a decision for the hospital, not a default for this project. The
  "what-if" cutoffs above are the information that decision needs.

## Action taken and not taken

Taken: race and gender removed from the model inputs; audit is reproducible with
one command (re-run it after each retrain); the recall floor (0.50) applies to
promotion.

Not taken: no group-specific thresholds; no re-weighting. Both are documented
options, not silent defaults.
